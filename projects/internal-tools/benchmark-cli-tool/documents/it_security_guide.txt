ACME ENTERPRISE IT SECURITY, ACCESS CONTROL, AND DATA COMPLIANCE GUIDE
Document Reference: SEC-SOP-2026-02
Effective Date: February 10, 2026
Target Audience: All Employees, Contractors, and Third-Party System Administrators

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This Standard Operating Procedure outlines the technical controls, authentication mechanisms, and acceptable usage criteria required to safeguard company information assets, customer data repositories, and intellectual property from unauthorized access, loss, or cyber threats.

2. IDENTITY MANAGEMENT AND PASSWORD RULES
Strong authentication forms the primary boundary of our corporate security model.

2.1 Complexity Requirements
All corporate account passwords must be at least 16 characters in length and include uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special symbols (e.g., !, @, #, $). Passwords containing dictionary words, common sequences, or employee names are flagged by automated identity checks and rejected.

2.2 Rotation Schedule
Corporate credentials and access tokens must be rotated every 90 days. Users are prohibited from reusing any of their previous 10 passwords upon rotation. Account locks are triggered automatically after 5 consecutive failed authentication attempts.

3. ENDPOINT PROTECTION AND ACCESS CONTROL
Corporate endpoints and personal devices used for work must comply with strict physical and operational security standards.

3.1 Hard Drive Encryption
All corporate laptops and mobile workstations must be encrypted using BitLocker (for Windows endpoints) or FileVault (for macOS endpoints). Encryption keys are managed centrally by the IT Infrastructure team. Manual disabling of encryption tools is considered a critical security violation.

3.2 Screen Lock and Idle Policy
Unattended workstations must be locked after 5 minutes of inactivity. Screen savers must be configured to prompt for account password or biometric verification upon resume. Leaving unlocked devices unattended in public spaces will result in immediate revocation of remote access privileges.

3.3 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is mandatory across all SaaS tools, internal portals, cloud environments, and VPN nodes. Authentication must utilize hardware security keys or time-based one-time password (TOTP) authenticator applications. SMS-based multi-factor authentication is strictly deprecated and disallowed.
