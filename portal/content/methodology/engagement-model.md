# Engagement Model

How an engagement with BenchmarkTech works, from first conversation to final handoff.

## Engagement phases

### Phase 1 — Scoping (1–2 weeks)

Every engagement begins with a scoping phase, whether the client comes to us with a defined problem or a general aspiration. We do not accept work we haven't scoped ourselves.

Scoping produces:
- A written problem statement agreed to by both parties
- A proposed solution approach with major decisions made explicit
- A delivery plan with milestones and review points
- A statement of what is out of scope
- A fixed-price or time-and-materials proposal

Scoping is billed. We do not do free discovery work on complex technical problems.

### Phase 2 — Discovery (1–3 weeks, built into most engagements)

For any engagement that involves building, we run a structured discovery phase after contract signing. This is not scope creep — it is the mechanism that makes fixed-price delivery possible.

Discovery outputs are documented in the [Discovery Playbook](/playbooks/discovery).

### Phase 3 — Delivery

We deliver in increments, not in a single handoff at the end. Each increment produces a demonstrable output reviewed by the client before we proceed.

**Weekly rhythm:**
- Standing async update (written, 200–400 words): what happened, what's next, any decisions needed
- Decision requests escalated within 24 hours; standing weekly call optional at client preference

**Definition of done for each increment:** a stated deliverable is complete when it meets the acceptance criteria defined in scoping, verified by the client.

### Phase 4 — Handoff

A delivered system is not done until the team that owns it can operate it without us. Handoff includes:

- Documented runbooks for day-to-day operation and common failure scenarios
- An annotated architecture diagram with every dependency called out
- A 60-minute walkthrough session with the people who will own the system
- A 30-day support period for questions and minor adjustments

## How we handle scope changes

Scope changes are inevitable on technical engagements. Our rule: **no undocumented scope changes.**

When new work is identified:
1. We flag it in writing in the next async update
2. We estimate the impact (time, cost, schedule)
3. The client decides: add it, defer it, or drop something else to make room
4. We document the decision

This is not bureaucracy — it is how we ensure you don't receive a surprise invoice and we don't deliver less than you expected.
